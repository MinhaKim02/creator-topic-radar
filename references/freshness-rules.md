# Freshness rules

## Build a timeline

Record the research cutoff and timezone. For each candidate, distinguish:

| Date | What it means |
|---|---|
| Underlying event date | When the action, announcement, result, ruling, or change occurred |
| Source publication date | When a source published its account |
| Database record creation date | When an archive stored the record |
| Claim creation date | When someone formulated the claim |
| Substantive update date | When new evidence or a material development changed the story |

Use unknown or not applicable rather than assuming dates are identical. Publication can serve as the date of an announcement only when the source establishes that relationship. If a timestamp lacks timezone or only an approximate upload age is visible, preserve that limitation; do not manufacture precision.

## Classify the story

- **New event:** A concrete event occurred within the stated research window.
- **Old event + new development:** Identify both the original event and a recent ruling, earnings result, launch, regulatory action, filing, evidence, or relevant market reaction. Explain what changed.
- **Ongoing:** The issue remains active or unresolved, with an evidenced reason to discuss it now. Unresolved status alone does not mean a recent development occurred.
- **Commentary only:** A new article or video revisits existing facts without a material change.
- **Unclear:** Available evidence does not establish the event date or the claimed update.

Do not impose an unexplained universal freshness threshold. A weekly news brief and an evergreen educational brief have different needs. If the user gives no window, choose and disclose a reasonable one.

## Verify current status

For a current brief, check developments through the research cutoff. For a historical as-of brief, use only information that was available by that cutoff for the original assessment. A post-cutoff source describing an older event was not necessarily available at the time; locate a contemporaneous source or mark historical evidence insufficient. Do not use later corrections, outcomes, or view counts to rewrite what was knowable then. If later knowledge is useful and requested, label it separately as a post-cutoff update. Unknown source availability limits the as-of judgment.

Ask what actually happened, when it happened, what changed, and whether later sources supersede the account. Check for corrections, withdrawals, delayed launches, subsequent decisions, revised datasets, and changed availability that affect the core premise.

An older event can become relevant again for a concrete reason. Keep the chronology explicit: “The original launch was in May; a September repair program changes the ownership question.” Do not relabel the May launch as new.

If timing is central but unverifiable, mark the candidate provisional or decline to recommend it as a current event. An old topic may still be suitable for an explicitly evergreen brief, with accurate dating.
