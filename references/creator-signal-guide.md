# Creator signal guide

## Separate evidence roles

Use creator-led videos as audience-interest signals. Use appropriate primary sources and authoritative reporting for factual verification. Keep official promotional channels and institutional/news channels distinguishable from independent creators. Their visibility can provide context, but it is not a substitute for creator sampling.

Where accessible, expand from video to channel, recent uploads, adjacent creators, and recurring questions. State whether adjacency came from recommendations, platform links, or ordinary search. A search result is not evidence of recommendation-graph traversal. Disclose when search visibility or inaccessible platforms constrain the sample.

## Maintain a creator watchlist and 72-hour radar

For recurring monitoring, maintain a user-editable watchlist of relevant independent creators with canonical channel URLs, intended audience and program type. Verify channel identity before collecting. Review membership periodically using audience fit, publication cadence, and distinct questions contributed. Expand through relevant searches, recommendations only when actually visible, and user audience data; a fixed list alone does not establish ecosystem-wide coverage. The same show, podcast/video versions, clips, cohosts, and syndications count as one program.

Inspect the last 72 hours first, then days 4–7 and, only if thin, days 8–14 with clear date labels. A newly uploaded explainer can cover an old event; date both. Store a dated, source-linked observation for each inspected video: canonical ID/URL, creator, title, published time, format/duration if visible, observation time, displayed views and provenance, access depth, event, concrete question, and discovery path. Distinguish title-only mention from an inspected argument. Group by event and *separately* by the same audience question. Two independent programs asking the same question within 72 hours are a useful convergence lead, not independent factual verification. Keep a one-program outlier or early upload labeled as such.

Compare with the previous accessible archive by video ID and normalized question: `first baseline`, `new upload`, `continuing`, `new shared question`, or `changed status`. Retain observation history rather than overwriting earlier snapshots. Without a prior archive, do not claim what changed since yesterday. Compare response with the creator's own similar-format, similar-age uploads only if historical observations support it; do not compare a new video's early count with mature lifetime totals, infer an age-matched ratio from them, or extrapolate views per hour. Mark unavailable baselines and reassess later. Comments can reveal audience questions but are neither representative polling nor factual verification.

Keep one compact dated private archive entry per run with cutoff/timezone, inspected and inaccessible sources, links, question groups, interest labels, verification status, and next check. Deduplicate repeated clips/observations within a run; on a quiet day, record coverage and `no new qualifying observation` instead of inventing candidates. The skill does not provide a scheduler, storage, platform APIs, or paid scraping access. Configure those separately for unattended monitoring; do not copy private archives into a public repository.

## Check sampling breadth

Adapt the sample to the audience, geography, subject, and research window. Include relevant source languages and a useful range of formats, such as daily briefings, focused event or company analysis, explainers, and topical discussion programs. Treat example channels as rotating seeds, not a complete roster. There is no universal minimum number of channels and no requirement to cover every language or format.

Keep a coverage ledger of programs actually inspected: language, region when relevant, format, episode/upload date, URL, events or questions observed, and access depth (body/transcript, description, or title only). Count inspected content separately from search hits and inaccessible links. A title can support that an event was mentioned; it does not establish the creator's argument. Expand gaps that could change the ranking, or state the actual sample and its limits. Stop when the requested shortlist has defensible facts, relevant interest evidence, and clearly disclosed gaps.

Daily briefings and multi-topic discussions can reveal timely events before individual videos accumulate substantial views. Extract the event first, then its audience-relevant question. Repeated coverage is an attention signal even when metrics are unavailable; it is not proof of exceptional video response.

## Record observations

For each useful video, record platform, creator/channel, title, URL, publication timestamp or approximate age, observed view count when available, observation timestamp, and where the metric came from.

Distinguish a platform-displayed count from a search snippet, cached page, rounded count, or third-party aggregate. Do not call an external aggregate a live platform count. Counts change after observation; a dated observation is not a permanent value.

When possible, inspect typical reach and a recent comparable upload sample. Note sample size, time range, format, and source. Use comparable formats and similar topics; avoid comparing Shorts with long-form videos without qualification. A subscriber count is not a view baseline. A few selected hits do not establish typical reach.

## Compare only what is comparable

Separate established creator reach from current-video performance. A well-established creator's new upload may be notable while its eventual performance is not yet judgeable.

For example, a two-day-old video with 300K views and a two-hour-old video with 80K views from a creator who often reaches 1M+ are not directly comparable. Do not downgrade the second solely on its current count.

Prefer age-matched observations when actually available. A current lifetime count for an old upload does not reveal its two-hour performance. Do not invent historical snapshots or assume linear view growth. Views per hour can be descriptive when age and counts are reliable, but it is not a validated performance forecast or a substitute for age matching.

If age-matched observations or a defensible baseline are unavailable, describe reach and observation limits separately. There is no universal minimum age at which every video becomes judgeable.

## Assess convergence

Assess convergence at two levels: independent programs covering the same concrete event, and independent programs discussing the same underlying question. Record each separately. Same-company mentions alone establish neither. Event convergence can motivate a new editorial angle, but does not establish that the exact proposed question is already widely discussed.

Count a program's full episode, Shorts clips, podcast and video versions, syndications, and multiple hosts as one program for convergence. Different episodes from that program add observations, not independent-program counts. Record the distinct programs actually inspected and the differing angles where useful. Identify shared source dependence or coordinated promotion when evidenced. Multiple reactions to one announcement show attention; they do not independently verify its claims. Keep language-specific evidence distinct when reporting geographic or language uptake.

One video can motivate further investigation. Do not treat it alone as established ecosystem-wide interest. No discovered videos can reflect search/access limitations rather than absence of demand.

## Express the result

- **Strong observed:** Multiple credible observations support interest, with scope and comparison limits stated.
- **Limited observed:** Some relevant evidence exists but is narrow or poorly comparable.
- **Early:** Recent uploads or an emerging discussion are visible, but performance is not yet judgeable.
- **Unassessed:** Access or data does not support an assessment.

These are explanatory labels, not calibrated probability bands. Do not promise virality, invent a numeric threshold, or rank candidates by absolute views alone.
