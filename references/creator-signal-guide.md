# Creator signal guide

## Separate evidence roles

Use creator-led videos as audience-interest signals. Use appropriate primary sources and authoritative reporting for factual verification. Keep official promotional channels and institutional/news channels distinguishable from independent creators. Their visibility can provide context, but it is not a substitute for creator sampling.

Where accessible, expand from video to channel, recent uploads, adjacent creators, and recurring questions. State whether adjacency came from recommendations, platform links, or ordinary search. A search result is not evidence of recommendation-graph traversal. Disclose when search visibility or inaccessible platforms constrain the sample.

## Record observations

For each useful video, record platform, creator/channel, title, URL, publication timestamp or approximate age, observed view count when available, observation timestamp, and where the metric came from.

Distinguish a platform-displayed count from a search snippet, cached page, rounded count, or third-party aggregate. Do not call an external aggregate a live platform count. Counts change after observation; a dated observation is not a permanent value.

When possible, inspect typical reach and a recent comparable upload sample. Note sample size, time range, format, and source. Use comparable formats and similar topics; avoid comparing Shorts with long-form videos without qualification. A subscriber count is not a view baseline. A few selected hits do not establish typical reach.

## Compare only what is comparable

Separate established creator reach from current-video performance. A well-established creator's new upload may be notable while its eventual performance is not yet judgeable.

For example, a two-day-old video with 300K views and a two-hour-old video with 80K views from a creator who often reaches 1M+ are not directly comparable. Do not downgrade the second solely on its current count.

Prefer age-matched observations when actually available. A current lifetime count for an old upload does not reveal its two-hour performance. Do not invent historical snapshots or assume linear view growth. Views per hour can be descriptive when age and counts are reliable, but it is not a validated performance forecast or a substitute for age matching.

If age-matched observations or a defensible baseline are unavailable, describe reach and observation limits separately. There is no universal minimum age at which every video becomes judgeable.

## Assess convergence

Look for independent creators discussing the same underlying question, not just mentioning the same company. Record how many distinct creators were actually inspected and the differing angles where useful. Exclude simple reposts and identify shared source dependence or coordinated promotion when evidenced. Multiple reactions to one announcement show attention; they do not independently verify its claims.

One video can motivate further investigation. Do not treat it alone as established ecosystem-wide interest. No discovered videos can reflect search/access limitations rather than absence of demand.

## Express the result

- **Strong observed:** Multiple credible observations support interest, with scope and comparison limits stated.
- **Limited observed:** Some relevant evidence exists but is narrow or poorly comparable.
- **Early:** Recent uploads or an emerging discussion are visible, but performance is not yet judgeable.
- **Unassessed:** Access or data does not support an assessment.

These are explanatory labels, not calibrated probability bands. Do not promise virality, invent a numeric threshold, or rank candidates by absolute views alone.
