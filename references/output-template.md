# Output template

Translate headings and judgment labels into the requested output language. Preserve source titles when useful. Use one compact card per candidate by default; a comparison table may supplement cards, but must not erase sources or caveats. Keep the detailed evidence ledger separate from the final selection. Expand only when requested or when a material uncertainty or tradeoff needs explanation.

## Brief research context

- Audience, scope, platforms, geography, time window, and stated assumptions.
- Research cutoff: date/time and timezone.
- For historical briefs, distinguish evidence available by the cutoff from separately labeled later updates.
- Discovery modes actually used, inspected sample breadth and material language/format gaps, and the accessible comparison history.
- Material access limits and unperformed checks.

State shared context once, not on every card. For a selected-topic archive, retain only context needed to interpret the topic and its evidence; do not automatically copy the entire research process.

## Default candidate card

- **Working title:** A clear provisional title without unsupported certainty.
- **Core question and explanation:** One question and a short evidenced answer or causal chain, not a script.
- **Selection reason:** Why now and why the intended audience may care, with a dated event or update and the relevant interest evidence. Distinguish same-event coverage from same-question coverage.
- **Key evidence:** Essential primary-source links and inspected creator/program links. Keep facts, attributed claims, analysis, and predictions distinguishable.
- **Important limits and decision:** State material uncertainty, essential scope, comparison-history limits, missing interest evidence, or a partial record match when they affect selection. Identify recommended versus provisional status.

Use dates and observation times where volatile evidence affects the recommendation. Missing history must remain visible even in a concise card. Do not omit a qualification that changes the meaning merely to shorten the output.

## Detailed research ledger

Retain these fields as separate research assessments. Show them when requested or needed to explain a decision; do not require every field in every final card.

- **Working title:** A clear provisional title without unsupported certainty.
- **Core question:** One question the video would answer.
- **What the video explains:** The evidenced answer or causal chain, not a script.
- **Why the audience may care:** A concrete consequence relevant to the brief.
- **Timeline:** Underlying event date; material update date; distinguish source, record, and claim dates where relevant. Mark unknowns.
- **Audience Interest:** Label and reasons. Link inspected creator videos; include publication age/date, metric provenance and observation time where available. Describe baseline, convergence, or early-signal limits.
- **Freshness:** Label, what actually changed, and why it matters now.
- **Factual Reliability:** Label; central facts/claims with epistemic status and linked primary sources or disclosed reporting fallback. Make scope and source conflicts visible.
- **Duplicate Risk:** Label; closest prior topic, semantic overlap, and comparison-set limits. Use unassessed when history is unavailable.
- **Short-form Suitability:** Label; why one question fits, needs narrowing, or cannot retain its essential caveats in roughly 60 seconds.
- **Key caveats:** Qualifications that the eventual explanation must retain.
- **Optional record/claim match:** Direct, partial, none, or unassessed; omit if irrelevant to the task.
- **Research gaps:** Unknowns, missing access, and what would resolve them.
- **Recommendation:** Recommend, provisional, or do not recommend, with reasons.

Place citations next to the statements they support. Do not cite a creator video for factual reliability merely because it discussed the topic. Never fill missing evidence fields with invented links, dates, or counts.

## Shortlist decision

Explain why the recommended candidates lead for this brief. Show fewer candidates if evidence is insufficient; say when none are defensible. Explain tradeoffs rather than producing a weighted total or promising performance. End at topic selection.

State the number of Recommend entries explicitly. Merge overlapping candidates before counting them. If useful, add a short, separate Provisional follow-up section with the missing evidence; these are not additional recommendations. Keep recommended and provisional entries together within the requested maximum unless asked for a larger backlog. Do not produce full cards for every rejected discovery unless the user requests an audit trail.

## Handoff after selection, when requested

- Selected topic and core question.
- Verified facts, separately labeled company/reported claims, and necessary scope.
- Key sources and research cutoff.
- Important caveats and unresolved questions.

Do not turn the handoff into narration, scene design, prompts, or a production plan.
